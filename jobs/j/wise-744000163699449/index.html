Principal Content Designer, Fincrime - AUP

London, GBDesignOn-siteposted 2d ago

Apply on Wise →

How do you help more people and businesses move money while keeping financial crime out? How do you make complex restrictions understandable, and help customers take the right next step? How do you challenge policies that create unnecessary barriers? These are some of the challenges you’ll work on as a Principal (IC) Content Designer in Wise’s AUP team, supporting our mission to build an open and fair financial system.

Your mission

You’ll lead content design for AUP, helping Wise support more legitimate customers and lawful activities while managing risk effectively. Our team is moving from a policy-first approach to a risk-driven one. That means questioning blanket restrictions, understanding the risks behind different activities, and designing proportionate responses. You’ll turn that strategy into clear policy and useful experiences, so customers understand what they can do with Wise, which conditions apply, and what to do when something goes wrong.

You’ll set the content direction and stay hands-on, working with Product Design, Product, Engineering, Operations, Compliance and other FinCrime teams. Your work will connect the public policy with what customers experience when they join Wise, move money or encounter a restriction. About

the role

The Acceptable Use Policy sets out how people and businesses can use Wise. The AUP team sits within the Servicing Tribe and FinCrime Chapter. Today, it focuses on monitoring transactions, with an ambition to address AUP risks across the customer lifecycle. Our priorities include enabling transfers to regulated crypto platforms, removing unnecessary restrictions on lawful, lower-risk activities, and exploring ways to serve businesses we currently restrict. We’re also improving how we detect risky activity and helping our Operations teams make effective decisions.

Content design is central to making these changes work. A shorter policy needs to remain accurate. A warning needs to explain what action a customer should take. A limit needs to be understandable at the moment it affects someone. And a customer using a personal account for business may need a practical route to the right account. We’re looking for someone who can make complex rules understandable, question the experience behind the words, and bring teams together around a clear direction.

What you’ll work on

  • Make new permissions and limits understandable. Design experiences that explain supported activities, eligibility and restrictions. For regulated crypto transfers, this could include guidance, scam warnings and clear explanations of transfer limits and next steps.
  • Help customers avoid repeated violations. Improve contextual in-app warnings and supporting communications so customers notice them, understand the issue and know how to respond.
  • Explore alternatives to unnecessary account closure. Help customers understand the difference between personal and business use, and test journeys that guide eligible customers towards a business account. Support discovery into onboarding and exception journeys for currently restricted business use cases.
  • Support consistent operational decisions. Partner with Operations on clear guidance, terminology and customer communications as controls and review processes evolve, including those supported by machine learning and AI.
  • Measure and improve the experience. Use research, customer feedback and behavioural data to test understanding and action. Evaluate whether content helps reduce repeated violations and avoidable friction, supports legitimate customer growth, and maintains appropriate safeguards.
  • Rethink the public AUP. Lead the content design of a shorter, clearer policy that focuses on illegal and high-risk use cases and explicitly distinguishes personal and business restrictions. Work with Compliance and other policy owners to make it accurate, accessible and useful.
  • Build lasting content governance. Establish clear ownership, review and approval processes, and a way to keep policy and product communications aligned as rules change across activities and markets.

Who you’ll be designing for

People and businesses carrying out legitimate activities. They need to understand whether Wise supports their use case, which conditions apply and how to keep moving money without unnecessary disruption. Customers who misunderstand a rule or use the wrong account type. They need timely, specific guidance and a practical next step. You’ll help distinguish a correctable mistake from behaviour that requires a firmer response. People who may be vulnerable or exposed to harm. You’ll bring empathy and care to sensitive journeys, including scam warnings and restrictions that could affect someone’s access to financial services.

Customers whose activity presents significant risk or breaches our terms. You’ll design firm, clear and proportionate communications, working with Compliance and Operations to explain what we can disclose and what happens next. Across these experiences, you’ll help Wise communicate fairly without assuming that a restricted activity or an automated alert makes someone a bad actor.

What you’ll bring

  • Hands-on content design experience across complex product journeys, with evidence that your work improved customer understanding and outcomes.
  • The ability to set strategy at Staff level, influence decisions across teams and deliver detailed content yourself.
  • Strong information architecture and plain-language skills, including experience turning complex policies or requirements into usable content.
  • Sound judgement in sensitive situations: knowing when to reassure, when to be direct and how to explain consequences fairly.
  • Experience using research and data to challenge assumptions, prioritise work and evaluate results.
  • The ability to build sustainable content practices and help colleagues make better content decisions.

Experience in financial services, compliance or another regulated environment would be useful. What matters most is your ability to learn a complex domain, ask thoughtful questions and design clear experiences within its constraints. Heads up, this probably isn’t for you if: Your experience is mostly in marketing content, advertising, copywriting, journalism or social media, and you have no hands-on experience of content design or working in UX. This role needs someone who can shape policy, journeys and product decisions as well as write the words.
