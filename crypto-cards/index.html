crypto cards

Crypto cards, narrowed down

107 cards · 84 on Visa, 41 on Mastercard · from the open dataset of 398 neobanks · updated 2026-11-25

short answer107 crypto cards are tracked here: 41 self-custodial, where you hold the keys until you pay, and 66 custodial, where the issuer holds the balance. 71 publish a cashback rate. Only one can be ordered with no identity check at all; 31 more come from wallets that work without one, where only the card needs it.

Most lists of crypto cards rank them by a cashback number. That number is the least reliable thing about a card, and it is not the first thing to decide. Start with who holds the money, then with whether the card works where you live, and only then with what it pays back. Each list below is one of those questions, answered from verified fields, with no affiliate links.

Common questions

What counts as a crypto card here?

A payment card from a product that is crypto-native (a hybrid fiat-and-crypto app or a web3-native wallet) or that spends stablecoins directly. A bank card whose app merely lets you buy bitcoin is not counted. By that rule 107 of the 398 neobanks tracked here issue one.

Is there a crypto card with no KYC?

One in this dataset can be ordered with no identity check. Cards run on Visa or Mastercard and are issued by a regulated partner that has to verify the cardholder. 31 products come close in a different way: the wallet works without verification and you verify only if you order the card.

Who holds the money on a crypto card?

It depends on the custody model, which is the first thing to check. On the 41 self-custodial cards the crypto stays in your own wallet until the moment of payment. On the 66 custodial ones the issuer holds the balance and you hold a claim on it, so the issuer’s solvency and terms matter in the way a bank’s do, usually without deposit insurance.
